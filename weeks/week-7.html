Week 7 - Variability & Model Fit

How Good Is This Model?

For three weeks we have been reading estimates — slopes, intercepts, group differences. This week we read the other half of the output: the numbers that tell you whether the model is worth anything at all.

The core idea is simple arithmetic. Start with how wrong you would be using nothing but the mean. Fit a line. Whatever distance the line covers is the model; whatever is left is the residual. That single split gives you R², the F test, and the residual standard error — three numbers you have been glancing past since Week 5.

NoteThe sentence to watch for this week

“Statistically significant” and “explains a lot” are different claims. With 400 people you can be very confident about a slope that accounts for 6% of the variance. Both facts are true, and a careful results section says both.

We will also meet the first real trap in model building: R² can never go down when you add a predictor, even a pointless one. That is why adjusted R² exists, and why it is the number to watch once your models have more than one thing in them.
